# <ins>Code of Conduct</ins> // <ins>Mod Submission Acceptance Policy</ins>

## 1. Open-Source Mandate

1. ### If your mod contains custom DLL(s) or executable file(s), the DLL(s) or executable file(s) must be open-sourced and the source code must not contain any obfuscation. Submission requests that do not comply will be denied.
   1. If your mod is an addon, e.g. a Blueprinter Aircraft Mod, or a Voice Pack, or similar, and <ins>does not contain any custom DLL(s) or executable file(s)</ins>, there is no cause for concern.
   2. During periods of retroactive enforcement of the mod submission acceptance policy, owners of any mods that are found to be in breach of this clause will be contacted privately and asked to comply and will be given a reasonable grace period. Failing to take action to ensure compliance before the grace period expires will result in delisting the specific mod(s) that breach this clause.

## 2. Zero Tolerance Clauses

1. ### If your mod is found to be containing any of the following, all of your submissions will be delisted and all your future submission requests will be denied.
   1. Obfuscated code.
   2. Malicious code.
   3. Making unwarranted changes to the users' computers.
   4. Purposeful interference with Nuclear Option in any way so that it might not launch or might terminate during runtime, or any other way to degrade the user experience on purpose.
2. ### If custom DLL(s) or executable file(s) in your mod(s) release(s) are found to be inconsistent with their source code and these inconsistencies are found to be breaching Zero Tolerance Clause 2.1, all of your submissions will be delisted and all your future submission requests will be denied.
   1.  "*...inconsistent with their source code*" meaning: containing additional code that is not present in the available source code for the open-sourced DLL(s) and/or executable file(s).

## 3. Licensing, Copyright, Attribution, and Permission

1. ### If your mod uses, adapts, or contains any third parties' work, you must abide by those licenses. Failure to do so may result in the specific submission(s) that breach this clause being denied, or the specific mod(s) found to be breaching this clause to be delisted.[^1]
   1. The requirements you're likely to run into are:
      1. Giving credit to the original author.
      2. Licensing the code you used *or* your entire project under the same license.
      3. Making the source code you used freely available (already a requirement by [1.1.](#1-open-source-mandate) and [2.1.](#2-zero-tolerance-clauses)).
      4. Including a copy of the license in your repository/source code.

2. ### If you have been granted permission by Shockfront Studios to use in-game assets in your project, you must also display proof of permission. Failure to do so may result in your submission(s) being automatically denied.[^1]

3. ### If you intend on publishing your own version of someone's mod, one of the following must be true:
   1. The author gave you permission to do so.
   2. The mod is under a permissive license or copyleft license, and you exhausted all reasonable attempts to reach out to the author and they have not responded in at least seven days.
   3. The mod is under a permissive license or copyleft license, and the author seemingly cannot be contacted.
   4. The mod is under a permissive license or copyleft license, and a reasonable person would agree that the mod appears to have been abandoned.

## 4. Communication

1. ### If you are unsure about something, reach out and ask.
   1. It's better to *look* like an idiot for 60 seconds than it is to *be* an idiot indefinitely.
2. ### Be respectful, be patient, and keep an open mind.
   1. Don't be quick to judge people over text. Most social problems occur to due a breakdown of communication and it's virtually impossible to know how someone feels or what someone's intentions are over text messages. You might think you're in a heated argument with someone, while they might think you're both just hanging out and having a laugh.
3. ### Toxic behaviour will not be tolerated.
   1. This includes instigating arguments and/or hostility, personal attacks, harassment, threats, knowingly spreading misinformation with intent to deceive or slander, excessive antagonism, and deliberately provoking others for a reaction. We're all here for Nuclear Option.
4. ### Criticise ideas, decisions, and work. Don't criticise people.
   1. Insulting someone or making them feel bad is hardly productive. Instead, focus on the thing they did or said and provide advice on what could have been done better.
5. ### If you have a problem with someone, give them the respect of addressing it privately and try to clear things up.
   1. Public channels are not the place for it.
   2. There is likely a misunderstanding/disconnect that caused the problem in the first place. There are no social cues or body language in someone's text. Try to consider their possible perspectives and/or motivations.
   3. Reach out, open up, and be genuine. If you expect them to be vulnerable with you, it'll be far more likely to happen if you start by opening up and being vulnerable with them.

## Example Violations

> ### Example Violation of 3.1:
> Using a 3D model of an aircraft or a vehicle that was made by someone else and released under Creative Commons License, but not crediting the author or displaying the license in the project's repository.

> ### Example Violation of 3.1:
> Forking someone's project and publishing it without giving any attribution to the original author or failing to abide by the license they provided with their project.

> ### Example Violation of 3.2:
> Including ripped assets from the game in your repository.
